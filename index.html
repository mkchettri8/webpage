Research

My doctoral research centres on kinetic Alfvén wave (KAW) turbulence in the heliosphere. I develop and run pseudospectral simulations of modified Zakharov equations — governing the nonlinear coupling of KAWs with ion acoustic and magnetosonic waves — and validate the results against in-situ measurements from spacecraft missions. The goal is to understand how energy cascades from large MHD scales down to kinetic scales, where it is ultimately dissipated as plasma heating.

PhD Research Objectives

1

Numerical Simulations & Model Validation

Perform pseudospectral simulations of nonlinear plasma phenomena and validate against Parker Solar Probe, Voyager, and MMS observations.

2

KAW Turbulence in the Magnetosphere

Study turbulent signatures of kinetic Alfvén waves in Earth's plasma sheet boundary layer and magnetosheath using simulations and MMS data.

3

Nonlinear Wave Coupling in the Inner Heliosphere

Investigate coupled dynamics of KAWs and ion acoustic waves using two-fluid models and compare with near-Sun spacecraft data.

4

Beta-Dependent Spectral Analysis

Explore sensitivity of dispersive Alfvén waves to plasma beta, mapping spectral scaling across different heliospheric and magnetospheric regimes.

Methodology

Theoretical Models

Two-fluid MHD, modified Zakharov equations, kinetic theory, nonlinear Schrödinger formulations with Landau damping

Numerical Methods

Pseudospectral with 2/3-dealiasing, 4th-order Runge-Kutta, split-step Fourier, MPI-parallelised solvers

Observational Validation

Multi-mission spacecraft data (MMS, Parker Solar Probe, Voyager, Wind) via PySPEDAS; spectral and statistical comparison
