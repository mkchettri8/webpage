Research

My doctoral research centres on kinetic Alfvén wave (KAW) turbulence in the heliosphere. I develop and run pseudospectral simulations of modified Zakharov equations — governing the nonlinear coupling of KAWs with ion acoustic and magnetosonic waves — and compare the results against in-situ measurements from spacecraft missions. The central question is how energy cascades from large MHD scales down to kinetic scales, where it is dissipated as plasma heating.

PhD Research Objectives

1

Numerical Simulations & Model Validation

Pseudospectral simulations of nonlinear plasma phenomena validated against Parker Solar Probe, Wind, MMS, and Aditya-L1 observations.

2

KAW Turbulence in the Magnetosphere

Turbulent signatures of kinetic Alfvén waves in Earth's plasma sheet boundary layer and magnetosheath, combining simulations with MMS observations.

3

Nonlinear Wave Coupling in the Inner Heliosphere

Coupled dynamics of KAWs and ion acoustic waves from two-fluid models, compared with near-Sun spacecraft data.

4

Beta-Dependent Spectral Analysis

Sensitivity of dispersive Alfvén wave spectra to plasma beta, across magnetospheric and solar wind regimes.

Methodology

Theoretical Models

Two-fluid MHD, modified Zakharov equations, kinetic theory, nonlinear Schrödinger formulations with Landau damping

Numerical Methods

Pseudospectral with 2/3-dealiasing, 4th-order Runge-Kutta, split-step Fourier, MPI-parallelised solvers

Observational Validation

MMS, Parker Solar Probe, Wind, and Aditya-L1 data via PySPEDAS; spectral and statistical comparison
